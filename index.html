Trajectories

An Online Seminar Series on Inequality, the Life Course, and Politics

How do the social experiences we encounter throughout our lives shape the way we think and act politically? Trajectories brings together research on how social inequalities, social environments, and experiences across the life course shape political behaviour, attitudes, and preferences. We are particularly interested in research on political socialisation and on the processes through which families, schools, peers, workplaces, voluntary organisations, and other social contexts influence political orientations and behaviour.

About the Series

From political sociology, the series takes the premise that political behaviour has social foundations that can be studied systematically. From a life course perspective, it takes the insight that these foundations are not fixed, but develop through experiences and transitions over time. Political socialisation provides an important conceptual link between the two: rather than treating social background as a static determinant of political outcomes, it directs attention to the processes through which social experiences become politically consequential.

We welcome theoretically driven empirical research at different stages of development — work in progress is especially welcome, as are early-career researchers and doctoral students. The seminar is intended as a collegial space for constructive discussion, in which theoretical arguments are developed, empirical strategies questioned, and ideas revised through exchange.
