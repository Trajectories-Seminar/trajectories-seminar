      About the series

      Trajectories brings together research on how social inequalities, social environments, and experiences over the life course shape political behaviour, attitudes, and preferences. We are particularly interested in political socialisation, and in the processes through which families, schools, peers, workplaces, voluntary organisations, and other social contexts influence political orientations.

      The series draws on two traditions. From political sociology it takes Lipset's premise that political behaviour has social bases which can be studied systematically, along with Sartori's view of political sociology as a genuine hybrid of social and political explanation. A life course perspective adds that these bases are not fixed: they develop through experiences and transitions over time.

      We welcome theoretically driven empirical research on political participation, vote choice, attitudes, and preferences regarding policies, institutions, and political actors. The focus is mainly quantitative, though qualitative and mixed methods projects are equally welcome. We especially encourage doctoral students, early career researchers, and colleagues from groups underrepresented in academia.

      Present with us

      We would be happy to hear from researchers working on political socialisation, life course dynamics, inequality, or related questions of how social experiences shape political orientations and behaviour. Work in progress is welcome.
